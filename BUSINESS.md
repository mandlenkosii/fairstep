# FairStep: Business Case

**Every step fair. Every step on record.**

> FairStep helps small business owners run fair, fully documented disciplinary processes, so they stop losing CCMA cases on procedure.

Author: Mandla Sikhosana · Market: South Africa · Prices in ZAR, excl. VAT

---

## 1. The problem

### Who has it
Owners of South African small businesses with **5–50 employees**: spaza shops, restaurants, security companies, building contractors, small retailers. They have no HR department, no in-house labour-law knowledge, and no budget for a lawyer every time an employee arrives late, steals stock or fails to arrive for a shift.

### How often it happens
- The CCMA registered **193,069 referrals in 2024/25**, up by about 4,500 on the previous year. **51% were unfair dismissal disputes**, roughly 98,000 cases in one year. Private security and retail each made up 13% of referrals, and building 7% ([Business Day, 23 Sept 2026](https://www.businessday.co.za/news/2026-09-23-unfair-dismissals-make-up-more-than-half-of-ccma-cases/)).
- About **60% of the CCMA's caseload** has come from small, medium and micro enterprises (CCMA 2018/19, reported by [Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/large-scale-dismissals-continue-to-rise-in-labour-market-on-back-of-legislative-reform-4ir-2019-10-14)). When the CCMA and BUSA launched their small-business app, they put the figure at **80%** ([Engineering News, 2021](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/ccmabusa-launch-free-to-use-small-business-labour-advice-app-2021-11-22)).

### Why employers lose
A commissioner's first question is whether the process was procedurally fair. Many employers with legitimate cases lose **because their paperwork was completed incorrectly, lost, or not properly recorded** ([SME South Africa](https://smesouthafrica.co.za/why-employers-lose-ccma-cases/)). Fairness has to be investigated, documented, communicated and proved ([Polity, 2026](https://www.polity.org.za/article/winning-isnt-always-being-right-2026-10-27)).

### What it costs today
- **Compensation.** In 2026 the Labour Appeal Court held that gross procedural unfairness does not make a misconduct dismissal *substantively* unfair. The remedy for procedural unfairness is still generally **compensation** (*Universal Product Network v Mbatsana N.O.* [2026] ZALAC 14; [Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2026/South-Africa/Employment-Law/employment-law-alert-8-june-can-gross-procedural-unfairness-mutate-into-substantive-unfairness), [Werksmans](https://werksmans.com/bad-process-doesnt-make-a-good-dismissal-bad-lac-draws-a-clear-line-between-procedure-and-substance/)). The LRA (s194) allows unfair-dismissal compensation of up to **12 months' remuneration**. For a worker earning R6,500 a month, that is up to **R78,000**, which can be more than a spaza shop's monthly turnover.
- **Time.** Owners lose days to conciliation and arbitration, plus the hours spent preparing letters from scratch.
- **Advice.** Labour consultants typically charge per hearing, roughly R3,000–R8,000 (*assumption A2, to validate*).

Most of our customers don't have one, so FairStep **is** their HR person. The product therefore supports several case managers per business: the owner invites a manager, and both run cases. The language assumes no legal training.

## 3. The solution

**FairStep is a mobile-first case manager that guides a small business through a fair disciplinary process, blocks steps that would make it unfair, generates the letters, and exports a timestamped, CCMA-ready case bundle.**

How it works:
1. **Employee register + incident log.** Every incident is logged the day it happens, with witnesses and evidence (photos, statements).
2. **Guided workflow.** Incident → investigation → notice of hearing → hearing → outcome → appeal (optional), following the [2025 Code of Good Practice: Dismissal](https://www.acts.co.za/labour-relations/n3470_notice_no__3470_of_2025), which replaced Schedule 8 of the LRA on 4 Sept 2025. Unfair moves are **blocked** with a plain-language reason, for example: "The employee must get at least 2 working days' notice to prepare."
3. **Documents.** Notice of hearing, written warning, final written warning and outcome letter are generated as PDFs from the case data.
4. **Consultant review.** The owner sends the case to their labour consultant, who comments, requests changes or signs off.
5. **Audit trail + CCMA bundle.** Every action is timestamped and attributed. One tap exports the whole case as a single PDF.

**Positioning:** process guidance and record-keeping. **Not legal advice.** Consultant review is built into the product.

## 9. Legal position and POPIA

- **Not legal advice.** FairStep provides process guidance and record-keeping. Rules and notice periods are configurable and must be verified by a qualified labour practitioner. This disclaimer appears in the app, in generated documents and in these docs.
- **POPIA.** The **business is the responsible party**, and **FairStep is the operator** processing employee personal information on its behalf. We collect only what a case needs: name, employee number, job title, start date, and optional work contact details. We don't collect ID numbers, home addresses or health data. Evidence lives in private storage buckets that only the business and its assigned consultant can access.
- **Liability.** FairStep blocks steps that are *procedurally* incomplete under the Code. It does not decide whether a dismissal is *substantively* fair; that judgement stays with the employer and their consultant.
